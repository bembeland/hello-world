📈 Distribution curve — xG by gameweek (GW1–38)

Definition (read this first): one “team-match” is one club playing one match. League-wide xG Scored per team-match = total xG created in the gameweek ÷ 20. xG Conceded per team-match = total xG allowed ÷ 20. League-wide these two totals must be equal — every chance created by someone is conceded by someone else — so the purple and teal league lines overlap by definition. That is not a bug; it is the mirror at the heart of football. To show the two perspectives separately, the chart also splits home scoring (= away conceding) and away scoring (= home conceding), which genuinely differ week to week. Pink shading is the projected 68% band (±1σ), lighter pink the 95% band (±1.96σ), widening the further we project.

🌊 Fourier series — removing noise, honestly

Is it doable? Yes — in two distinct jobs, with one hard limit. A Fourier series writes a wavy series as a sum of sines/cosines: xG(t) = a₀ + Σ [aₖ·cos(2πkt/P) + bₖ·sin(2πkt/P)]. Low frequencies = slow waves (seasonal drift, congestion cycles, form waves); high frequencies = week-to-week noise. Keeping only the low frequencies is a low-pass filter — it denoises. Projecting the slow waves forward is a harmonic projection — but only if the wave's period has actually been observed.

What was applied here (gold lines):

  • Denoising GW1–11: discrete Fourier low-pass keeping frequencies k = 0, 1, 2 over the 11-week window (periods 11 and 5.5 gameweeks). It explains 65% of the weekly variance (R² = 0.65) and smooths GW1–11 to: 1.66, 1.44, 1.29, 1.39, 1.60, 1.62, 1.37, 1.09, 1.08, 1.36, 1.65. With only 11 points and 5 effective parameters this is descriptive smoothing, not proof of a cycle — a 5.5-week “wave” here is two bumps, not a law of nature.
  • Projecting GW12–38: harmonic regression on the true seasonal periods (P = 38 and P = 19 gameweeks), ridge-regularised (λ = 5) so the wave amplitude stays small. Result: a gentle wave between 1.37 and 1.50 instead of a flat 1.41 — i.e. Fourier, properly disciplined, mostly agrees with the mean and adds only a mild seasonal tilt (slightly higher around GW17–19 and GW36–38 in this fit; do not over-read the phase, it is fitted on 11 points).
  • Why not the naive Fourier projection? We tried it: an unregularised P = 38/19 fit on GW1–11 is ill-conditioned (we have seen only 29% of one season-cycle, so the sine/cosine columns almost cancel) and extrapolates to an absurd xG of 2 → 35 by mid-season. That model was rejected and is shown here as a warning: Fourier will happily invent a wave you have not observed. Any Fourier projection of a full-season period needs at least one full season — ideally several — of history to be identifiable.

Residuals: what's left after the wave is removed

The bars below are actual − Fourier-smoothed for GW1–11. These residuals are where one-off shocks would live. Largest: GW6 +0.23 (still high even after smoothing — a genuinely chance-rich week), GW2 +0.20, GW10 +0.17; negative: GW5 −0.22, GW1/GW3/GW8 ≈ −0.11.

Can a residual tell us “that was an injury / a red card / the weather”?

Not from league-wide xG alone — and anyone who claims otherwise is over-fitting a story to a wiggle. With 11 league-level data points you cannot separate five causes (weather, pitch, congestion, red cards, injuries): that is more explanations than observations, and a league average hides which team/player was affected (the ecological fallacy). What Fourier does give you is a clean, dated list of anomalies to investigate. Attribution then becomes doable — at match-team level (220 rows in GW1–11, 760 over a season), like this:

The full recipe, if we had that data: (1) fit the Fourier/harmonic baseline per team-match with team attacking & defensive strengths; (2) add the covariates above; (3) a residual spike is only “explained” if the covariate was present, in the right matches, with the right sign and a plausible size — and it survives out-of-sample testing on later gameweeks. Until then, GW6's spike is a question (“what happened that week?”), not an answer. That discipline is the difference between Fourier analysis and Fourier astrology.

🔔 The bell curve underneath

Normal fit to the 11 gameweek averages: N(μ = 1.41, σ = 0.26). About 68% of gameweeks should land between 1.16–1.67 xG per team-match, and 95% between 0.90–1.92. Ticks under the curve are the real GW1–11 values — note GW6 out on the right tail and GW8 on the left. Across individual clubs the spread is similar in centre but personal: team xG For per match ranges 1.00 (Hull) to 1.87 (Brighton); xG Against 0.97 (Arsenal) to 2.03 (Crystal Palace) — same league mirror, very different lives inside it.

💡 What the curve says

  • The middle is normal life. 7 of the first 11 gameweeks sit inside the 68% band. A “typical” Premier League week in 2026–27 creates about 1.4 xG per team — roughly 28 xG, and about 27–28 goals, across 10 matches.
  • Tails win headlines. GW6 (36 goals, 1.85 est. xG/team) was a right-tail festival; GW8 (19 goals, 0.97) was the left-tail grind. Neither redefines the league — the curve expects roughly 1 week in 20 outside the 95% band.
  • Scored = conceded, league-wide. There is no league-wide “attacking era” without a simultaneous “conceding era”. Clubs move along the curve; the league’s mirror total only moves when chance quality itself moves.
  • Projection ≠ prediction of a week. GW12–38’s flat line is the centre of the distribution, not a forecast that every week will be 1.41. Expect most weeks inside the pink, a few flirting with the edges — that is the forecast.
  • Finishing bends reality, briefly. After GW11 Chelsea had 20 goals from 13.74 xG (+6.26) and Forest 9 from 16.91 (−7.91) per Squawka/Opta. The curve’s quiet promise is regression toward the middle.

🧪 Methodology & sources

Actual goals (exact): every GW1–11 scoreline was counted from FBref’s 2026–27 Premier League Scores & Fixtures (110 matches, 303 goals). Per-GW goals/match: GW1 3.00, GW2 3.20, GW3 2.30, GW4 2.90, GW5 2.70, GW6 3.60, GW7 2.60, GW8 1.90, GW9 2.10, GW10 3.00, GW11 3.00.

From goals to xG (transparent estimate, never silent invention): free per-gameweek Opta xG is not published as a clean series — FBref lost its Opta advanced feed in Jan 2026 and Understat has been offline since 2 Nov 2026 (see sources). So GW xG is estimated as GW goals ÷ 20 × (season Opta xG ÷ season goals), where season Opta xG = 310.69 (sum of all 20 clubs’ xG For in Squawka’s Opta table after GW11; the xG Against column sums to the identical 310.69, a good internal check) and season goals = 303. Calibration factor = 1.025. Cross-checks: this gives GW11 = 3.08 xG/match vs Squawka’s published GW11 Opta match values summing to 3.26/match (−5.8%); a different model (prematchstats) puts the season at 2.93 xG/match and xgstat’s GW1 at 3.43/match — xG models differ, which is exactly why the estimate is labelled est. everywhere.

Projection (GW12–38): fit N(μ, σ) to the 11 GW-average xG values (μ = 1.412, sample σ = 0.257). Projected centre is flat at μ ( fitted slope −0.016/GW is negligible). Forecast σ grows with horizon h gameweeks ahead as σ·√(1 + h/11), so the 68% (±1σ) and 95% (±1.96σ) bands widen — by GW38 the 95% band is roughly 0.48–2.35. Dates for GW12–38 are the published fixture-calendar gameweek start dates (subject to change for TV).

Club backtest & learning loop: GW1–11 club predictions in the club table are generated match-by-match with an expanding window — strengths for GWk use only goals for/against from GW1…k−1 (GW1 = league prior), strengths based on goals per team-match (league 1.377) with a 4-match league-mean prior blended in so 1-game samples cannot explode, same venue factors. Reported per club: outcome hits /11, mean absolute error for goals scored and conceded, biggest miss. The future (GW12–38) model then applies a learning correction: (actual ÷ predicted goals in the backtest), shrunk 50% toward 1 and capped to [0.85, 1.18], separately for scoring (×corrF) and conceding (×corrA). “Why might it be off?” tags are rule-based investigation prompts (finishing above/below prediction ≥1.2 goals, conceded above/below ≥1.2, high/low-event games, outcome misses) — they are not confirmed VAR/weather/red-card/injury causes; confirming any of them requires the match-level event and weather data listed in the Fourier section, which is not yet joined. As GW12+ are played, the honest workflow is: add the result, read its tag, verify or reject the tag from the match report, then refit — corrections change in the open, never silently.

Fourier layer: (i) in-sample denoising = DFT low-pass, keeping rFFT bins k = 0,1,2 of the 11-point GW series (numpy rfft/irfft, zeroing bins ≥3), R² = 0.65, residuals listed in the table; (ii) forward wave = harmonic regression on periods 38 & 19 GW with ridge penalty λ = 5 on the harmonic coefficients (intercept unpenalised), giving GW12–38 values in the table (range 1.37–1.50). The unregularised version of (ii) was computed, produced a nonsensical 2→35 xG extrapolation, and was discarded — reported here so the choice is auditable. Fourier terms capture only smooth, periodic structure; congestion from cup/European fixtures, red cards, injuries and weather are not periodic and enter only as match-level covariates in the residual analysis described in the Fourier section, which requires event data not in the league-wide series.
